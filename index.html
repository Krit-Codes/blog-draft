The case against

Six reasons AI homework backfires

F

The struggle is the learning

That annoying feeling of being stuck on a problem is literally your brain building new connections. Skip the struggle and you skip the upgrade.

No pain, no brain.
F

Tests don't have Wi‑Fi

Homework is practice for exams. When the test lands on your desk and there's no chatbot to ask, the knowledge has to already be in your head.

ChatGPT isn't sitting the final for you.
D

AI is confidently wrong

Chatbots make up facts, invent sources and botch maths while sounding totally sure. If you can't do it yourself, you can't spot the mistakes.

Fake quotes = real zero.
F

Teachers can tell

Your teacher has read your writing all year. An essay that suddenly sounds like a corporate press release stands out, and AI detectors plus "explain this to me" questions do the rest.

"Delve" gave you away.
D

You forget your own work

If you didn't write it, you can't remember it. Try explaining your last AI essay out loud without looking. Hard, right?

Wait, what was my thesis?
F

Thinking is a muscle

Writing, problem-solving and focusing for more than 30 seconds all get weaker when you never use them. You're going to need them for the rest of your life.

Skip brain day, regret it later.

Actual science, not just vibes

Scientists hooked brains up to sensors. ChatGPT users lit up the least.

In 2025, researchers at MIT's Media Lab had students write essays three ways: with ChatGPT, with a search engine, or with nothing but their own brains, while EEG headsets measured brain activity.

Lowest engagementThe ChatGPT group showed the weakest brain connectivity of all three groups.
Couldn't quote itMany ChatGPT users struggled to recall a single line from the essay they had just "written".
Got lazier over timeAcross sessions, the AI group leaned on copy-paste more and more.

Study: "Your Brain on ChatGPT", Kosmyna et al., MIT Media Lab (2025). Small, early study, but the direction is pretty clear.

To be fair

AI isn't evil. Just use it like a tutor, not a ghostwriter.

Brain‑growing uses

  • "Explain this concept in a simpler way."
  • "Quiz me on chapter 4."
  • "Here's my answer. Where did I go wrong?"
  • "Give me a practice problem like this one."

Brain‑shrinking uses

  • "Write my 500‑word essay on the Cold War."
  • "Solve all 20 of these maths problems."
  • "Rewrite this so it doesn't sound like AI."
  • "Answer these reading questions for me."
